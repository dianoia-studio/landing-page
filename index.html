the thinking is the important part

For centuries, writing and sketching freely have been a medium for rich thought. AI is building a new medium mostly through typing and voice — but the freeform one, the paper and the whiteboard, is still nearly untouched.

On the page, the end result is rarely what matters. What matters is the thinking that happened across time: the route tried first, the dead ends, the erased attempts, the arrow that finally joins two ideas.

Dianoia is an experiment in letting AI see that — the whole canvas as it develops — and asking it to hold back. It watches quietly, offers hints only when asked, and gives you a post-mortem when you're done. The reasoning stays yours.

— Serene, day 0 of 90

a fragment of scratch work

try induction on n? base case n=1 ... assume k, show k+1 ...
wait — what breaks for n=2? ↳ you erased this at 4:12. it was closer than you thought.

The observer doesn't interrupt. It notices — the fast strokes, the slow ones, the crossing-out, the long pause — and saves what it sees for afterwards.

The feedback after a completed attempt is worth more than hints during it. Practice does not make perfect; deliberate practice does.

διάνοια

διάνοια — from διά (through) + νοῦς (mind): to think something through, step by step.

Its cousin: διάγραμμα — from διά + γράμμα: that which is drawn through. Thought-through and drawn-through share a root.

In Plato's Republic, dianoia is the mode of the geometers — reasoning that leans on visible diagrams to reach invisible truths. It is, quite literally, thinking with scratch work.

  1. The canvas. A browser whiteboard you can draw on with a stylus, with an AI observer watching the scratch work evolve.
